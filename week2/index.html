On this day, the 1st of September 2026, I ask you this; should Chaco Canyon
be opened for mineral, oil, and gas extraction?

The Trump administration plans to rescind a 10-mile buffer zone barring drilling
and mineral extraction around Chaco Culture National Historical Park in northwestern
New Mexico. In addition, the Bureau of Land Management plans to revoke a 2023
order closing about 336,404 acres near Chaco Canyon to drilling and mining for 20 years.

Also known as Tsébiyaanii' áhá by the Navajo people, Chaco Canyon is
an environmental and historic wonder of the American Southwest

  • It is revered as an ancient civilization site
  • Spiritually significant site of the Navajo nation, and many of the
    surrounding pueblos
  • Stands as a culturally important landscape
  • Acts as a preservation site for many keystone species of the
    surrounding areas

This piece of land has remained untouched for thousands
of years, but has recently drawn notice of regional natural resource developers as
a potential source of:

  1. Oil
  2. Gas
  3. Trace minerals

Many in opposition to this development argue that the cost of degrading the local
environment greatly outweighs the short-term benefit of accessing oil and gas reserves.
On the opposite side, many in defense of this act would say that the development of
Chaco Canyon would bolster local job economies and benefit the nation as a whole.

The local tribal governments of New Mexico are under a tight deadline for a
14-day commenting period about this issue, but have started banding together in a
stand against any resource development of this important site. It seems like the ultimate
fight on this issue will be dealt with in a court of law, potentially from lawsuits, or
hopefully from some form of negotiation. Since this is a site on tribal lands, it stands to
reason that the reigning say-so on this matter should lay with the tribal government and its people.
